Wrapped Ether (WETH) is native ETH locked in a wrapper contract and re-issued as an ERC-20 token that always redeems 1:1. In practice you wrap ETH so it can move through contracts that only accept ERC-20, and unwrap by calling withdraw to get the same ETH back. This page is an independent reference for how wrapped ETH works — it holds no keys and takes no custody of anything.

What is Wrap WETH?

WETH is Ether in ERC-20 form. ETH is Ethereum's native currency and predates the token standard, so it doesn't expose the transfer, balance, and approval functions most smart contracts expect. The wrapper contract fixes that: it holds your ETH and credits you an equal WETH balance that behaves like any other ERC-20 token — transferable, approvable, and usable in delegated transfers.

Every WETH is backed by ETH sitting in the wrapper contract, and you redeem through the same contract at par. It is not a staking derivative like stETH, not an interest-bearing receipt, and wrapping it does not move anything between networks — it's the same asset, same chain, different interface.

Getting in and out

Wrapping is a single transaction. Connect a self-custodial wallet, confirm you're on the intended network, and verify the WETH contract for that network — on Ethereum mainnet (chain ID 1) the canonical WETH9 contract is 0xC02aaA39b223FE8D0A0e5C4F27eAD9083C756Cc2. You send ETH to the contract's payable deposit() function; when the transaction confirms, the contract credits your wallet an equal WETH balance. There's no conversion fee and no slippage — only network gas, paid in native ETH, so keep some unwrapped.

Unwrapping is the reverse: call withdraw(amount) on the same contract. It burns that WETH balance and returns the identical amount of native ETH. One transaction each way, complete when the transaction confirms — timing just depends on congestion and your gas settings.

Swapping it on a DEX

Once you hold WETH, trading it for another token is a standard ERC-20 swap. Two things differ from the wrap itself: the route now has a pool fee and price impact, and the DEX needs an approval before it can move your WETH. The approval is a separate transaction that grants the router a spending allowance — read the allowance amount before signing it, since unlimited approvals are convenient but widen your exposure if that contract is ever exploited.

Before confirming the swap, check the quoted minimum received and the price impact on your trade size. Many DEX interfaces also offer to wrap ETH inline as part of the trade; that's the same deposit() mechanism, just bundled into the route.

The token vs its native asset

WETH and ETH are the same value with different capabilities. Ether is the native asset: it pays gas, it's what you receive from the wrapper on withdrawal, and it's what most wallets treat as your base balance. WETH is the ERC-20 representation: it works with approvals, allowances, and any contract written to the token standard, but it cannot pay gas.

The practical rule: hold ETH for spending and gas, hold WETH only while a contract or pool actually needs the ERC-20 form. Since redemption is always 1:1, there's no yield or penalty for either — the choice is purely about interface, not economics.

Costs and gas

The canonical wrapper charges nothing. Direct wrapping and unwrapping have no protocol conversion fee and no slippage — you put in 1 ETH, you get 1 WETH, and vice versa. Your only cost is Ethereum network gas, paid in native ETH, for the deposit or withdrawal transaction.

Costs change when WETH goes through a DEX swap instead of the wrapper. There you pay network gas plus the pool's fee, and large trades relative to pool depth add price impact. Those are properties of the exchange route, not of WETH itself — the wrapper remains the fee-free path between ETH and WETH.

Is Wrap WETH safe?

The canonical mainnet WETH9 contract is about as simple as contract design gets: it has no owner, no administrator, no proxy, and no privileged minting function — it can only issue WETH against ETH actually deposited. It's open source and has been formally verified for important accounting and solvency properties, and you can inspect the verified code and balances directly on the WETH contract page.

None of that makes the activity risk-free. The real exposure is usually at the edges: fake wrapper contracts on other networks or phishing sites, approvals granted to malicious routers, and simply sending transactions on the wrong chain. Open-source and verified describes the canonical contract — it says nothing about the lookalike you reached from a bad link. Verify the network and the address every time.

Problems and fixes

Balance shows zero after wrapping. Almost always the wrong network in the wallet — WETH contracts are per-chain, so switch the wallet to the network where you wrapped, or add the correct WETH token contract for that network so the balance displays.

Unwrap fails or can't be submitted. Gas is paid in native ETH, and if you wrapped your entire balance you have none left to pay for the withdrawal. Send a small amount of ETH to the wallet first. Swap fails at the trade step. Check that the approval transaction actually confirmed and that the allowance covers the trade size. Sent WETH to an exchange as 'ETH.' They are different assets operationally; only the recipient's support can recover that, if at all.

WETH FAQ
What is WETH?

WETH (Wrapped Ether) is native ETH deposited in a wrapper contract and re-issued as an ERC-20 token. It exists because many smart contracts only accept ERC-20 tokens, and native ETH doesn't implement that standard.

Is WETH the same as ETH?

Same value, different form. One WETH always redeems for one ETH through the wrapper contract, but ETH pays gas and WETH follows the ERC-20 interface. Wrapping doesn't bridge anything or change the underlying asset.

How do I convert WETH back to ETH?

Call withdraw(amount) on the same wrapper contract from the wallet holding the WETH. The contract burns that WETH balance and returns the identical amount of native ETH in one confirmed transaction — you need a little ETH left for gas.

Is WETH always 1:1 with ETH, and how is it backed?

Yes — each WETH is backed by ETH held inside the wrapper contract, and redemption is at par. You can verify the backing yourself: the contract's ETH balance and WETH supply are both visible on public trackers like WETH on CoinGecko and block explorers.

What does it cost to wrap ETH?

The canonical contract charges no wrapping or unwrapping fee and there's no slippage — the only cost is network gas in ETH. Costs only appear when you swap WETH on a DEX, where pool fees and price impact apply.

Is the WETH contract safe?

The canonical WETH9 contract is open source, has no owner or privileged minting, and has been formally verified for key accounting properties. That reduces contract risk but doesn't remove it — you still must verify the network, the contract address, and every approval you sign, because phishing and lookalike contracts remain common.

Notes before you wrap weth

Wrapping is the boring part of DeFi, and boring is good. One transaction in, one transaction out, same value both ways — the discipline is all in the checks, not the mechanics.

The common mistake is trusting the ticker instead of the contract: searching 'WETH' in a wallet or DEX and picking whatever shows up, which is exactly how lookalike wrapper tokens on sidechains and phishing routes collect deposits.

Before you sign:

  • Match the contract address for your network — on mainnet that's the canonical WETH9 — never the token name or logo.
  • Keep native ETH unwrapped for gas; wrapping your full balance leaves you unable to pay for the unwrap.
  • On a DEX route, read the minimum received and price impact, and check the allowance size on any approval before confirming it.
